This is a letter signed by 25 Fields medalists about the fast-paced “progress” that AI models are making in the world of mathematics.

 > “Over the course of generations, [research mathematics] has built [...] **tools to comprehend the mathematical landscape**. [...] Famous problems have often served as landmarks and lighthouses against which one can **measure an improved understanding of this landscape**. [...] the mass production at faster and faster pace of "true/false" statements could destroy fertile ground instead of breathing life into new ideas.” (emphasis mine)

A (famous) unsolved problem is merely a testament to our lack of understanding of a certain area of mathematics.
To arrive at the solution, first we must study further, deepening our understand of that area of mathematics.
If an oracle gives us a rushed final answer without the body of work that usually backs such an answer, we lose out on _everything else_ we'd be getting from the process of getting to the solution ourselves.
